Kairo

Privacy policy

Effective 2 September 2026. Applies to Kairo for iPhone, including the TestFlight beta.

Kairo reads your activity from Apple Health, turns it into a character that levels up, and races your character against your friends. This page says exactly what is read, who can see what, and how to delete all of it. Nothing here is sold, used for advertising, or shown to anyone you have not chosen to race with.

2. What we collect

2.1 From Apple Health, with your permission

Kairo asks Apple Health for read access to eight kinds of data. It never writes anything to Apple Health, and it asks for nothing beyond this list.

Steps and walking or running distance
Score your Motion. Distance is also used as a cross-check that a step count is real.
Active calories
Score your Body.
Exercise minutes
Shown to you in your day, and used to adjust how your Motion score is judged. Not a score of their own.
Sleep
Score your Mind. Only read where your device provides it, typically an Apple Watch. A night you typed into Apple Health by hand is kept out of scoring.
Heart rate and resting heart rate
Your Strain figure. Shown only to you, never scored, never shown to a squadmate.
Workouts
Your Challenges, and to confirm a strength session was real before it counts towards Body.

Under the Data Privacy Act this is sensitive personal information, and we treat it as such.

How it is stored. Movement is stored as hourly totals for your own local day, for example "412 steps between 9 and 10 in the morning on 2 September": steps, distance, active calories, exercise minutes and an average heart rate per hour. Sleep is one total per night, with Apple's flag for whether it was typed in by hand. Resting heart rate is one figure per day. A workout is one record per session: when it started and ended, how long it lasted, its distance and calories, and Apple's numeric code for the kind of activity. We do not store routes, locations, workout names, or anything else from Apple Health.

2.2 What you tell us

  • A character name. It does not have to be your real name, and we suggest it isn't. It is the name your squad sees.
  • Optionally, your height, weight and year of birth, entered in Settings. Kairo never asks for them during sign-up and never blocks you on them. Birth year backs the estimate of your maximum heart rate behind the Strain figure. Only you can ever read them.
  • Squad names, and the name and description of a Battle you create for your squad.
  • Your decision on sharing daily totals with your squad, and your quest difficulty if you set one.
  • Your time zone, taken from your device, because your day runs from midnight to midnight where you are.

2.3 From Sign in with Apple

Signing in gives us an account identifier and an email address, which may be Apple's private relay address rather than your own. We do not show it to anyone, and we do not send mail to it. Apple also offers your name at sign-in; Kairo does not keep it, because your character is named separately.

2.4 What the app generates

  • Daily scores for Motion, Body and Mind, and the level, XP, Mastery and streak that follow from them.
  • Squad membership, invite codes, each finished day's race standings for your squad, and which quests, Challenges and Battles you completed.
  • A push notification token for your device, if you turn notifications on, and a log of which notifications we sent you and when.
  • Usage events: that the app was opened, which onboarding steps were completed, that your first sync and first score arrived, that you looked at the race or the day's details, and that your character reacted to something. Two events record a failure, with the error message, so a bug that is invisible to you is at least visible to us. No usage event carries a health figure.

2.5 What we do not collect

No location. No contacts, photos or microphone. No advertising identifier. No analytics, advertising or crash-reporting software from any other company is in the app. This website sets no cookies and runs no scripts of its own.

3. Who can see what

This is the table above, spelled out.

Your squadmates always see your character's name and look, your level, your streak, your Mastery, and where you placed in the day's race and on the squad's board. To place you, their app receives the day's score and per-stat bands the ranking is computed from, together with a flag if a day tripped our cheating checks.

Your squadmates see your daily totals only if you have both agreed. Steps, distance, active calories and minutes slept for the day are shown for a member who has turned sharing on, and only to a viewer who has turned it on themselves. Nobody can see anyone's totals without showing their own. If you have not agreed, your squadmates see your bird with no position and the words "not sharing", and you see the same of everyone else. You are asked when you create or join a squad. To withdraw, email us and we switch it off from our side; your totals stop appearing to squadmates at once.

One figure is shared without that agreement. While your squad is fighting a Battle, everyone on it sees the squad's pooled calorie total for the day, one number for the whole squad. Your own share of it stays behind the agreement above. A shared fight you cannot see the state of is not a shared fight, and joining one is itself a decision to take part.

In a squad of two, that pooled number can be worked backwards. Subtracting your own contribution leaves your partner's. There is no version of a shared total that avoids this, so we are naming it: in a squad of two, your daily calorie total is effectively visible to the other person while a Battle is running.

Your squadmates never see your hour-by-hour movement, any timestamp of when you moved, your heart rate, your workouts, your pace, your height, your weight, your birth year, or your email. None of these is behind a setting. There is no code path that returns them to anyone but you.

That separation is enforced by the database, not by the app. Squad data is served by a small number of read-only functions, each deciding for itself what it may return and to whom, and the sharing agreement is applied inside them, per person. A modified copy of the app cannot obtain anything those functions will not hand over. Hourly movement is treated as the most sensitive thing here on purpose: it reveals when you sleep, when you work and when you are away, which is a great deal more than a daily total does.

4. Why we process it, and on what basis

Scoring your day and racing your squad, which is the service
Your consent, given when you connect Apple Health on a screen that lists everything read, and necessary to provide what you asked for.
Showing daily totals to squadmates
Your separate, explicit consent, asked when you create or join a squad, and withdrawable as described in section 3.
Checking that a day's activity is real
Our legitimate interest in a race that means something.
Sending you notifications
Your consent at the iOS permission prompt, revocable in iOS Settings at any time. At most one daily digest, plus a message when a Challenge or Battle is completed.
Recording the two failure events
Our legitimate interest in the app working.

Consent to health data is asked for in context, never during sign-up. If you decline, Kairo still runs; your scores stay at zero.

5. Where it goes

Supabase hosts our database and server code, in Singapore (Amazon Web Services, region ap-southeast-1). Supabase processes data only on our instruction, as a Personal Information Processor.

Expo's push service and Apple's notification service deliver notifications. They see your device token and the text of each message. A message can name your character, the day's result, your place in the race, XP earned, or the distance or calories a Challenge was cleared at. It never contains your step count, sleep or heart rate.

Vercel serves this website and the invite page. It keeps ordinary web server logs and sees no app data.

Apple operates Sign in with Apple, Apple Health, and the TestFlight beta program. What Apple collects through those is covered by Apple's own privacy policy, not this one.

Some of these providers operate outside the Philippines. We remain accountable for your data wherever it is processed, as the Data Privacy Act requires.

We do not sell your data. We do not share it with advertisers, data brokers or analytics companies. We do not use health data for advertising or marketing, and we do not use it to train machine learning models. Apple's HealthKit rules forbid the first three independently of this policy, and we have no interest in any of them.

6. How long we keep it

For as long as your account exists. There is no rolling window: the character is a record of everything you have done, and deleting part of it would delete part of the character.

To delete everything, open Settings in the app and choose Delete account. It takes effect immediately and needs no email. We erase your profile, your health data, your scores, your streaks, your squad memberships, your device tokens, your notification log, and your quest, Challenge and Battle completions. If you led a squad, leadership passes to another member first, so your squad is not deleted with you. If you were its last member, the squad goes too.

Three things survive a deletion, and none of them can be linked back to you:

  • A Battle you created for your squad keeps its name and description, with no record of who wrote it, so the squad's fight does not vanish with you.
  • A finished race day keeps its standings for the rest of the squad, as account identifiers with no name attached. Once your account is deleted, nothing links that identifier to you.
  • Usage events are kept with the account identifier removed, so they count towards how the app is used and towards nothing else.

Our host keeps routine database backups for up to seven days. Deleted data leaves those on that schedule and is not restored from them.

If you revoke Kairo's access in iOS Settings instead of deleting your account, new data stops arriving at once. Data already scored stays until you delete your account.

7. Your rights

Under the Data Privacy Act you have the right to be informed, to object, to access your data, to correct it, to have it erased or blocked, to data portability, and to damages. In practice:

  • Access and portability: email us and we will send you everything we hold about you in a machine-readable form, within the period the law allows.
  • Correction: your character name, height, weight and birth year are editable in the app.
  • Erasure: delete your account in the app. It is immediate.
  • Withdrawing consent to health data: iOS Settings, Privacy & Security, Health, Kairo.
  • Withdrawing consent to sharing daily totals: email us. See section 3.
  • Notifications: iOS Settings, Notifications, Kairo.
  • Complaints: write to us first if you like, and to the National Privacy Commission of the Philippines regardless.

8. Children

Kairo is not for anyone under 13. We do not knowingly collect data from children. If you believe a child has an account, email us and we will delete it.

9. Security

Access is enforced at the database row: your rows are readable by you. The app itself holds no permission to write health data or scores at all. Every write goes through server code that checks who you are first, and every score is recomputed from the stored data rather than adjusted in place. Data is encrypted in transit and encrypted at rest by our host.

No system is perfectly secure. If a personal data breach occurs, we will notify the National Privacy Commission and the people affected within the period the Data Privacy Act requires.

10. The beta

Kairo is currently distributed through Apple's TestFlight, by invitation. Everything on this page applies during the beta. Two things are specific to it:

  • Beta data may be reset when the public version ships. Scoring is still being tuned, and history recorded under an older rule may be cleared rather than carried across. We will say so in the app before it happens.
  • Feedback and crash reports you send through TestFlight go to Apple first and are shared with us under Apple's TestFlight terms. Feedback you send by email comes to us directly.

11. Not medical advice

Kairo turns activity data into a game. It is not medical advice, not a training programme and not a diagnostic tool, and it knows nothing of your medical history. Consult a doctor before starting or changing an exercise routine, and stop if you are in pain. A broken streak is a game mechanic; an injury is not.

12. Changes to this policy

We will post changes here and update the date at the top. For anything that changes what your squad can see or what we collect, we will tell you in the app before it takes effect.
